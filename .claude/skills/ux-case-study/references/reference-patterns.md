# Reference patterns

Structural breakdowns of the two reference case studies. These are paraphrased notes on **how** they work. Do not reuse their wording.

- Cindy Ly, *Polimeter*: https://www.cindyly.design/work/polimeter
- Michelle Liu, *NASA JPL*: https://www.liumichelle.com/project/nasa/full

---

## Polimeter (Shape A: deep dive)

A shipped, responsive data-visualization web product that tracks political promises.

| # | Section | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Sticky anchor nav | Lists all sections so readers can jump | — |
| 1 | Hero | 3-screenshot strip, title, colored tag chips (Shipped / Data Viz / Responsive / Consumer Web), TL;DR with 2 metrics + live link, meta row: role, timeline, team by function, tools | Short |
| 2 | Background | One paragraph under a thesis subtitle: the product outgrew its interface | ~1 para |
| 3 | Problem | Subtitle gives a count + method (four problems, two audit tracks). Notes the two methods (design audit + expert methodology consult). 4 hover cards (bold label + one line + image). 2 user segments (journalists, citizens) with engagement level + needs | Medium |
| 4 | Process | Subtitle names a value tension (easy to scan vs deep enough to trust). Steps `01`–`04`: (1) information hierarchy restructure into 3 tiers, (2) chart explorations with early vs final captions + a **bolded breakthrough** reframing the axis, (3) click-to-expand before/after, (4) usability testing with a domain-expert pull quote | ~40% of page |
| 5 | Final product | Per-page subsections, each with a before/after toggle + short rationale. Design system (type, status badges, color tokens). Tablet + mobile breakpoints | Visual-heavy |
| 6 | Impact | Launch + stakeholder validation, a sentence on the designer's own role, a handoff note, 2 big stat callouts | Short |
| 7 | Reflections | 2 H3 lessons, one paragraph each: domain knowledge as design material, and an honest admission that external validation is still missing | Short |
| 8 | Close | Casual sign-off + "Up next" with 2 case study cards (with status tags) | — |

**Craft to borrow**
- Interactive affordances (hover cards, toggles, expanders) replace long explanation. In static form, use side-by-side images + captions.
- The problem is framed on two tracks (usability *and* risk of misrepresenting data). Show both the user pain and the stakes.
- The design system + breakpoints are shown as proof of handoff quality.
- The tradeoff is stated plainly: simplify without compromising the methodology.
- Voice: first person, active verbs, 2–4 sentence paragraphs, sparse bold.

## NASA JPL (Shape B: body of work)

A year-long creative/design role at JPL's in-house DesignLab. Four public projects out of 20+ (the rest are under NDA).

| # | Section | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero meta | Logo, title, 4-column meta (Date, Team, Role, Location), hero video |
| 2 | The mission | A one-line purpose statement for the whole role |
| 3 | Intro | A short warm welcome, the NDA note ("20+ other projects"), a thank-you |
| 4 | `00` Table of contents | Clickable project cards |
| 5 | `01` Comms banners | Mission (recruiting events, commissioned by Communications) → **Design research**: studied the archival graphics standards manual → **Process**: 3 directions presented, director picked two → **Final**: both sets approved and produced |
| 6 | `02` Career pop-up | Mission (labwide event, 6,000 employees) + a tight constraint (one week from brainstorm to print, with dates) → digital / print / mockups → 6 posters, each category paired with an archive image for recognition |
| 7 | `03` Robotics trading cards | Mission (help researchers assemble robot teams by terrain) → **By the numbers** (131 cards, 5 environments, 6 months) → drafts: color + icon system for at-a-glance environment ID → **Iterate, iterate, iterate**: months of accuracy checks with researchers → playful aside (field site visit) → final: printed in-house, delivered, collaborator thanked |
| 8 | `04` Holiday card + ornament | Mission (sent to all staff + lawmakers, 10,000 run) → ideation: chose multiple missions over one flagship because of launch uncertainty (a decision driven by a constraint) → selection by the Director's Office → production back-and-forth → physical result |
| 9 | Reflections | Themed heading ("post-mission debrief"), 3 emoji H3 lessons that are full sentences, each with a short paragraph |
| 10 | Close | "FIN" + a themed sign-off + "Also check out…" 2 cards |

**Craft to borrow**
- The **repeating mini-structure** per project makes a long page easy to scan.
- **Eyebrow labels** (small caps above titles) mark each beat of the story.
- **Constraint up front** (deadline, audience size, print run) makes small projects feel substantial.
- **Scope numbers as stats** when there are no outcome metrics.
- **The real-world ending**: every project closes with the physical or shipped result.
- **A light theme metaphor** in labels and the sign-off only.
- **Personality**: one playful aside per page and gratitude to collaborators.
- Reflection headlines *are* the lesson, written as imperatives.

## What both share

- Scannable hero with role/timeline/team meta
- Numbered sections/steps
- Very short paragraphs; visuals do most of the explaining
- First person, warm, confident, plain
- Stat callouts
- Decisions explained with a reason
- Short, honest reflections
- A close that routes to more work
